TechPotam — a short history

From One Instruction to the Edge of the Solar System.

In eighty years we went from a single punched card to machines that think alongside us — and we still haven’t stopped reaching. Scroll to see how we got here.

Scroll to begin

1943 — 1958

It began with a single instruction.

Before software had a name, it was a stack of punched cards — one hole, one instruction, one step closer to a machine that could follow a thought. ENIAC filled a room to do what a calculator now does from a pocket.

1957 — today

Then we taught it to speak our languages.

Fortran, C, Python, JavaScript — every language traded a little machine efficiency for a little more human meaning, until code started to read almost like a sentence.

2012 — today

Now it reaches into everything we do.

AI reads scans doctors might miss, predicts weather days out, folds proteins that took decades to understand by hand, and drafts a first version of almost anything. It isn’t one invention — it’s a new layer under all of them.

Medicine Climate Language Design Biology Space

1977 — today

And together, we reached for the edge.

Voyager 1 launched in 1977 running on roughly 68 kilobytes of memory — less than this page. It is now over 15 billion miles away, past the edge of the solar system, still sending data back on code written before most of us were born.

Voyager 1 — still transmitting, still beyond the edge.

What’s next

We’re still writing the instructions.

Every product we build stands on this — eighty years of people teaching machines to help us reach further. We just get to add the next few lines.
